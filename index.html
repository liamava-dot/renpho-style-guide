Writing Principles

  • Show, Don't Tell — Use product design images and specs, not emotional slogans
  • USP First — Highlight unique selling point features, not abstract benefits
  • Concise & Precise — Remove storytelling/emotion-driven content, focus on features
  • Consistency — Terminology, format, capitalization unified throughout

"Clinically Proven" Guidance

What it sounds like: strict clinical trials on large samples. What it actually means: anywhere from 1 person to a million have tested the product. Compliance Team rejects this term; seek Global Marketing approval first if you wish to use it.

Scales, Wearables & Non-Medical Devices

Permitted (under FDA General Wellness guidance)

DO "Track your trends. Take control of your wellness."
DO "Empowers healthy aging through daily cardiovascular tracking."
DO "Proactive wellness management for a balanced lifestyle."

Not Permitted

DON'T Diagnose hypertension / high blood pressure
DON'T Detect heart disease or cardiovascular issues
DON'T Determine if you have a medical condition
DON'T "Longevity" claims
DON'T Medical-grade accuracy / Clinical precision
DON'T "98% accuracy of a DEXA scan" (cannot conflate correlation with accuracy)

General Rule

Claims must remain broad (e.g. "track trends for heart health") and must NOT diagnose conditions. Confirm all promotional documents with PM and compliance department.
